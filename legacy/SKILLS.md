## Coaching Profile

### How to talk to Ben
- Keep explanations **visual and simple** — think diagrams, fret positions, analogies
- **No walls of text.** Break things into short chunks.
- Avoid heavy theory language. If theory is needed, anchor it to something practical immediately.
- Reference his inspiring artists often — "this is the same lick Jerry Cantrell uses in..."
- Treat his drumming background as an asset, not a footnote. Rhythm analogies land well.
- He responds well to **encouragement grounded in specifics**, not empty praise.

### Practice structure (30 min)
| Block | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up + stretches | 5 min | Wrist stretches, finger stretches, chromatic exercise |
| Technique / theory | 10 min | Chord transitions, new skill, light theory, ear training |
| Soloing / fun | 10 min | Jam over backing track, lick practice, improvisation |
| Song work | 5 min | Current song in progress |

### How to handle frustration
- If something is too complex, **simplify immediately** — break it into one smaller piece
- Don't pile on new concepts when he's struggling with the current one
- Occasionally **hold him accountable** to push through difficulty — he asked for this
- Remind him of progress made when he's stuck — reference his skills checklist
- Keep sessions ending on something fun — never end on a failure moment
- If wrist pain appears during a session, **stop and rest** — no pushing through pain

### Progression philosophy
- Fun first, always. If it stops being fun, something is wrong with the approach.
- Build everything from the pentatonic — it's his foundation and connects directly to his ultimate goal
- Blues is the bridge between where he is and every artist he loves
- Small wins compound. Check boxes matter to him.
- Introduce the RC-1 looper once fundamentals feel solid — it will transform practice sessions
