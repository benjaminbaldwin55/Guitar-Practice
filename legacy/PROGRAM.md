## Every Session — Non-Negotiable

### Wrist & Finger Warm-Up (5 min)
Always do this before playing. Your left wrist will thank you.

1. **Wrist circles** — 10 slow rotations each direction
2. **Prayer stretch** — press palms together in front of chest, hold 15 sec
3. **Reverse prayer** — backs of hands together, fingers pointing down, hold 15 sec
4. **Finger spreads** — spread all fingers wide, hold 5 sec, repeat 5x
5. **Chromatic crawl** — frets 1-2-3-4 on each string, one finger per fret, slow and clean

### Week 1 — Technique & Chord Refresher

**Session structure:**
| Block | Focus |
|---|---|
| 5 min | Warm-up (see above) |
| 10 min | Technique basics + chord name refresher |
| 10 min | Pentatonic noodling over Am backing track |
| 5 min | Learn the chord shapes for "About a Girl" |

**Technique basics to nail this week:**
- **Fret hand:** Thumb behind the neck (not hooked over), fingers curved like holding a ball, press close to the fret (not in the middle)
- **Pick hand:** Relax your grip — you don't need to strangle it. Light and loose.
- **Tension check:** Every few minutes, consciously relax your shoulders, jaw, and fretting hand. Tension is the enemy.

**Chord refresher — shapes you already know, names to reconnect:**
Start here. Play each shape and say the name out loud.

| Shape | Name | Fingers |
|---|---|---|
| Em | E minor | 2 fingers, strings 4+5, fret 2 |
| Am | A minor | 3 fingers, strings 2+3+4 |
| Dm | D minor | 3 fingers, diamond shape |
| G | G major | 3 or 4 fingers, open G shape |
| C | C major | 3 fingers, staircase shape |
| D | D major | 3 fingers, triangle shape |

**"About a Girl" — Nirvana (why this song)**
- Uses Em and G almost entirely — chords you already know
- Kurt Cobain plays it with a clean tone — great for practicing clarity
- It's cool, it's grunge, and it's genuinely fun to play
- YouTube search: *"About a Girl Nirvana guitar lesson beginner"*

**THR10II this week:** Set it to the CLEAN amp model, guitar volume at 50%, tone controls all at noon (12 o'clock). Just get a clear sound you like.

### Week 2 — Chord Transitions & First Lick

**Session structure:**
| Block | Focus |
|---|---|
| 5 min | Warm-up |
| 10 min | Chord transition drills |
| 10 min | Learn your first pentatonic lick |
| 5 min | "About a Girl" — intro + verse |

**Chord transition drill:**
Pick 2 chords. Set a timer for 1 minute. Switch back and forth as smoothly as you can — don't rush, prioritize clean over fast. This week: **Em → G**, then **Am → C**.

The goal isn't speed. The goal is that your fingers know where to go without you thinking about it.

**Your first intentional lick — the "blues curl"**
This is a 3-note lick used by almost every guitarist on your inspiring artists list.

On the A minor pentatonic (5th fret position):
1. Pick the 8th fret, B string
2. Bend it up slightly (half step) — push the string toward the ceiling
3. Let it ring, then release back down

That's it. That's a real lick. Jerry Cantrell does a version of this constantly.
YouTube search: *"A minor pentatonic bending lick beginner blues"*

### Week 3 — Strumming & Song Progress

**Session structure:**
| Block | Focus |
|---|---|
| 5 min | Warm-up |
| 10 min | Strumming patterns |
| 10 min | Pentatonic — combine noodling with your lick |
| 5 min | "About a Girl" — chorus + full run-through attempt |

**Strumming patterns — visual:**
Think of your strumming hand like a clock pendulum. It never stops moving down-up-down-up even when you're not hitting strings.

Basic pattern for About a Girl:
```
D = down stroke
U = up stroke

Pattern: D  DU  UDU
Count:   1  2+  +3+
```

As a drummer, think of D as your kick and U as your snare ghost notes. The hand keeps moving; you just choose when to make contact.

### Week 4 — Connect & Consolidate

**Session structure:**
| Block | Focus |
|---|---|
| 5 min | Warm-up |
| 10 min | Light theory: why Am pentatonic works (see below) |
| 10 min | Jam — back track + your lick + noodling, combined |
| 5 min | "About a Girl" — full song, start to finish |

**Why the pentatonic works — the visual explanation:**
Picture the A minor pentatonic scale as 5 "safe notes" that always sound good over a song in A minor. Think of it like a key that opens certain doors. The backing tracks you've been using are built on chords from the A minor family — Em, Am, Dm, G, C. The pentatonic scale is made from the same DNA as those chords. That's why it sounds right. You're not guessing — you're using the same building blocks as the song.

No memorization needed. Just know: **A minor pentatonic = home base for most of your favorite music.**
