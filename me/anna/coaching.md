# Coaching Anna

## How to talk to her
- Straight feedback. Say what's off and how to fix it. No sugarcoating.
- She's a trained musician (piano, violin). Don't over-explain music; explain
  *guitar*. She knows rhythm, practice discipline, and how to read music.
- She learns by ear first, then by watching. Lead with sound and video
  links; diagrams second. Standard notation is fine to show her.

## What to lean on
- **Her ear.** Ear training and call-and-response will come naturally and
  feed her "write music by ear" goal. Bring them in early.
- **Her voice.** Singing while strumming is the main goal. Build it in
  layers: strum without looking, strum while counting out loud, strum while
  humming, then sing.
- **Her discipline.** She's used to daily practice and working a piece
  until it's mastered. Songs with clear milestones suit her.

## Watch out for
- Too hard, too monotonous: those are what make her lose interest. Keep
  blocks short and varied, and get her into real songs fast.
- She practices after dinner with low energy. Keep the session easy to
  start; the hard part goes in the middle, never the end.
- Pick grip: she asked for help. Check it in her first few warm-ups.
- Long nails on the fretting hand: fret with the pads, a little flatter.
  Some chords will buzz until nails are short; say so plainly, don't nag.

## Stats
- Turned down at her request: show her personal bests and what she can do
  now that she couldn't before, not streaks or weekly counts.

## Placement
- She knows a few chords and remembers some plucking. Same chord path as
  Ben, but expect her to move through the first pairs quickly.
